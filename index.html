How to read the village

Q-day is the hypothetical point when a quantum computer can recover a signing key from its public key before the key or transaction stops being useful. The consequences below depend on what each key is allowed to do.

What decides the house?

Material records the migration work the map has evidence for today. Each placement identifies the decisive reason. A chain can change its own signing rules and still depend on another network to settle transactions.

Straw
A critical protocol or parent-chain dependency still needs migration. Chains awaiting individual review also start here and carry an explicit evidence label.
Stick
A specific migration path is proposed for an identified exposure. Bitcoin’s draft soft fork addresses long-lived public keys. Activation and post-quantum signatures remain outstanding.
Brick
Substantial post-quantum protection is deployed on mainnet. The review must name which users and critical functions it protects and what exposure remains.
Bunker
Evidence covers account signing and recovery, consensus, proofs, settlement and critical control keys wherever applicable. The review must also account for legacy keys and existing users.

These are editorial categories. A proposal can cover just one exposure. Unknowns remain visible, and a bridge link alone does not establish a dependency for an entire chain. The wolf applies the scenario defined for each material.

Which networks already use post-quantum signatures?

Quantus uses ML-DSA for native account signatures. Mining rewards have a separate proof-based withdrawal path. QRL has used XMSS signatures on its original mainnet since 2018. Neither network had an entry meeting the map’s $5M DeFi TVL cutoff in the 18 November 2026 snapshot. Quantus architecture ↗ · Quantus onchain evidence ↗ · QRL mainnet history ↗

Algorand is on the map. Its Falcon accounts can protect a holder’s signatures, and Falcon State Proofs attest to chain history. Consensus voting still uses Ed25519, and committee selection still uses an elliptic-curve VRF. That consensus work determines its current Straw placement. Algorand’s deployment and remaining work ↗

What can this chain change?

The agility assessment asks who can replace a cryptographic scheme, whether a network fork is required, and how existing users migrate while the network keeps running. NIST defines crypto agility in terms of replacing or adapting cryptography while preserving security and operation. The house assignments are this map’s own. NIST definition ↗

One house represents a chain with at least $5 million in tracked DeFi TVL. Houses grow at $100 million and $1 billion. Rows group parent relationships. The independent row includes chains with no recorded parent. Select a child house to highlight its parent, or a parent house to see its mapped connections. A solid branch marks settlement. A dashed branch marks another documented relationship. Aligned cable ends connect adjacent platforms. Family summaries distinguish the parent house from linked houses. A parent can have a row even when only its child meets the TVL cutoff. Other hosting relationships exist outside this sample. For example, Xai runs on Arbitrum One and fell below the cutoff in the 18 November snapshot.

The wolf adds a flattened chain’s TVL once per simulation. A cable describes a dependency. A hit counts only the house you blow down. The tally measures the TVL of affected houses; it cannot predict stolen funds. Switch off Wolf mode to reset.
