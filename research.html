Quantum migration research

Evidence snapshot · 18 November 2026

Made by the Starknet Foundation. This register is the source note for the village map: public documents, indexed cryptography and activity checks. The Foundation develops Starknet, which is one of the networks listed here.

This register covers all 83 networks in the village. We revisited primary sources for 23 entries. The remaining 60 carry forward the map’s existing evidence and identify the next research question. Each entry describes a specific exposure or milestone. A full network review also needs the other layers below.

What counts as evidence?

Start with who can spend, who confirms blocks, who can upgrade contracts, and which other network a user relies on. For each, record the algorithm, the people or contracts still using it, who can change it and what existing holders have to do. A protocol document establishes a design. An accepted proposal, client prototype, public testnet, opt-in mainnet feature and mainnet default establish different things. Record an activation block and a reproducible mainnet example before upgrading a deployment claim.

TVL determines eligibility and house size. It does not measure the balance controlled by exposed keys or predict how much an attacker could take. A source gap means the question remains open.

Solana in detail

  1. Ordinary wallets. The address of a normal keypair wallet is its Ed25519 public key. A capable quantum computer could recover its signing secret. Program-derived addresses have different authority rules.
  2. Native transactions. Anza and Firedancer prototyped Falcon. A program that checks a signature can protect assets under its own rules. Changing what the network accepts as a transaction signer requires coordinated client support. The specific Falcon verifier proposal closed unmerged.
  3. Existing holders. New wallets do nothing for keys already securing SOL, stake and applications. Jump describes a possible recovery proof using the original seed. Check eligibility for custodial, split-key and legacy accounts before assuming that every holder can use it.
  4. Consensus. Alpenglow activated in October 2026. Its BLS votes and aggregated finality certificates need a replacement with acceptable bandwidth and latency. Wallet Falcon support alone cannot deliver that.
  5. Network operations. Anza also identifies signed block fragments and stake-weighted network access. Test these paths and the removal of legacy authority before claiming end-to-end readiness.
